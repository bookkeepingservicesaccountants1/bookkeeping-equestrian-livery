Bookkeeping for Equestrian Centres and Livery Yards: Livery Fees, Feed and Farrier Bills

A livery yard sells space, feed and care by the week, while its biggest costs — hay, bedding, arena upkeep — arrive by the lorry load. The books need the same rhythm as the yard.

Livery fees: full, part and DIY

Full, part and DIY livery are three different products at three different prices. Record each horse's package separately, with extras — schooling, clipping, rug washes, trailer parking — as their own lines. Deposits and a month's notice money are liabilities until they are earned or applied, not income on arrival.

Feed, bedding and the muck heap

Hay, haylage, hard feed and bedding are bought in bulk and used across every horse. Bulk-buying discounts belong to the cost of the yard, and a simple stock tally at month end keeps the usage honest. Selling surplus hay or bedding to neighbouring yards is income of its own line.

Farriers, vets and third-party bills

Farrier and vet visits are often arranged by the yard but belong to the owner. Where you pay and recharge, record the recharge as money passing through — not as your income and not as your cost — with the invoice copied to the owner's monthly bill. Where the service is included in full livery, it is a yard cost.

Arena, fields and machinery

Arena surfaces, fencing, drainage and field maintenance are the yard's fabric: repairs are running costs, a new surface or a replacement tractor is capital. Keep the two apart, and keep grazing-only agreements separate from livery — they are different arrangements with different paperwork.
